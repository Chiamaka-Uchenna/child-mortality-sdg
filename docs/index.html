Who will miss the 2030 target for child survival?

Every country has committed to an under-five mortality rate of 25 or fewer deaths per 1,000 live births by 2030. This dashboard tracks progress for 196 countries, projects each one to 2030, and explores what separates countries that do well.

2030 tracker

Each country's trend over the last ten years is projected forward to 2030. The required pace is the yearly reduction needed from now on to reach 25. Projections are single values without an uncertainty range, so countries projected within five points of the target are marked too close to call, and each projection is also shown on a 5-year and a 15-year trend.

Method and limits

  • Data: World Bank World Development Indicators, 196 countries, 2000 onwards. Mortality estimates are from the UN Inter-agency Group for Child Mortality Estimation.
  • Projection: a log-linear trend over the last ten years, continued to 2030. Using a 5-year or 15-year trend instead changes the status of one country each time, and the count of off-track countries stays at 60 or 61. The UN's own uncertainty ranges around each mortality estimate are not carried into the projection.
  • Model: gradient boosted trees with monotonic constraints (more immunisation, water or sanitation can never raise predicted mortality), validated by holding out whole countries.
  • The model describes associations across countries, not causal effects. Estimates for countries without complete birth and death registration carry wide uncertainty, and national averages hide inequality within countries.
